# MG_191–MG_192: matched Pedro–experimental case study

We compared one Pedro AF3 prediction (job 250310_mgen_allbyall_1488, model 0; MG_192 chain C and MG_191 chain E) with the four main corresponding interfaces of 6RUT: assembly 1 A:B and G-2:H-2, and assembly 2 C:D and E-2:F-2. These are repeated experimental views of one protein pair, not four independent targets.

Sequence coverage explains much of the full-interface size difference. Full AF3 has BSA 5,474 Å², 1,437 residue contacts and 151 close contacts. Restricting both structures to corresponding observed residues gives AF3 BSA 2,788–2,791 Å² versus experimental 2,607–2,684 Å². AF3 then has 799–801 contacts versus 758–771, and 90 close contacts versus 100–105. Its close-contact fraction is 11.24–11.26% versus 13.19–13.62%, a remaining difference of 1.96–2.35 percentage points.

The main contact surface is well reproduced: experimental 8 Å contact recovery is 91.6–93.3%, and joint Cα RMSDs are 1.46–1.48 Å. Nevertheless, 29–37 experimental close contacts per copy leave the (3,4] Å interval: 8–11 shorten to ≤3 Å and 21–27 lengthen above 4 Å. Only one close-contact observation, in assembly 2 E-2:F-2, moves beyond 8 Å. Meanwhile, 19–22 other corresponding residue pairs enter the close-contact interval.

All experimental contact residues map reliably. The primary experimental close-contact pairs have complete expected heavy atoms, and the same lost-bin contacts remain after restricting both structures to common atom names. For example, MG_192 Asn807–MG_191 Gly705 changes from 3.46 to 5.30 Å, although its Cα separation changes by only −0.06 Å. This supports local atomic rearrangement rather than a missing-residue explanation for that contact.

Reference choice remains important. The common-residue fraction is also lower in AF3 against 6YRK (−2.24 percentage points), but higher against 8PBX (+0.65) and 8PBY (+1.20). Low-resolution structures and alternative contact surfaces are reported separately; four zero-correspondence 6RUT chain pairs and six homomeric pairs are not treated as genuine 1:1 matches.

Conclusion: this case separates coverage-related size differences from residual local geometry differences. It does not demonstrate a general AF3 bias, and even the direction of the close-contact difference changes with the experimental reference. No independence-based significance tests were applied.

Definitions: one contact per residue pair with minimum non-hydrogen heavy-atom distance ≤8 Å; close contacts satisfy 3 < d_min ≤4 Å. BSA = (SASA_A + SASA_B − SASA_AB)/2, with the established 1.4 Å probe and 100-point Shrake–Rupley calculation. Common-residue BSA is computed on trimmed coordinates and may change when solvent occlusion is removed.
